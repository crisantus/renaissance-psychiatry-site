Flexible care

You choose how you're seen

Appointments are offered by secure video or in the office. You select the format when you request your appointment, and you can change it between visits.

Availability varies by provider and appointment type. Whichever you pick, it's the same evaluation and the same provider continuity.

Telehealth appointments

Secure video visits from home, work or school, for patients physically located in a state where the provider is licensed.

  • Evaluations, medication follow-ups and psychotherapy by video
  • A private space and a reliable connection are all you need
  • No travel, and less time away from work or school

In-office appointments

Where available, the same evaluations, follow-ups and therapy can take place in person — and some assessments call for it.

  • Longer initial evaluations with unhurried conversation
  • Offered subject to provider availability
  • Your provider will say if a visit needs to happen in person

Thoughtful, individualized care

Conditions we commonly treat

A diagnosis is the beginning of a conversation, not the whole story. Below is what each of these tends to look like, what an evaluation pays attention to, and what treatment usually involves.

Attention

ADHD

Trouble sustaining focus, following through or sitting still — at school, at work or at home. Evaluation looks at how long the pattern has been present and where it actually causes difficulty.

Assessment, medication where appropriate, and practical structure for daily life.
Anxiety

Anxiety disorders

Generalized anxiety, social anxiety and worry that outlasts the situation that caused it, often with physical symptoms like tension, nausea or broken sleep.

Therapy, medication, or both — matched to how much it's interfering.
Mood

Depression

Low mood, loss of interest, fatigue and changes in sleep or appetite that persist for weeks. We look for what's driving it, including grief, medical causes and circumstance.

Medication management and psychotherapy, reviewed regularly for response.
Mood

Bipolar disorder

Periods of elevated energy, reduced need for sleep or impulsive decisions alongside depressive episodes. Accurate history matters here more than almost anywhere else.

Mood stabilization with close monitoring and a clear relapse plan.
Trauma

PTSD & trauma

Intrusive memories, avoidance, hypervigilance and disrupted sleep following a traumatic experience — recent or long past.

Trauma-informed care at a pace you set, with medication where it helps.
Obsessive-compulsive

OCD

Intrusive thoughts and repetitive behaviours or mental rituals that consume time and cause distress, even when you know they don't make sense.

Evidence-based therapy and medication, with realistic goals for daily life.
Anxiety

Panic disorder

Sudden episodes of intense fear with racing heart, breathlessness or dizziness — plus the anxiety of waiting for the next one.

Understanding the cycle first, then treatment to interrupt it.
Psychosis

Schizophrenia & psychotic spectrum

Changes in thinking, perception or motivation that make it hard to tell what's real or to keep up with usual routines.

Careful evaluation, medication management and coordination with your support system.
Sleep

Sleep concerns

Difficulty falling or staying asleep tied to a psychiatric condition — and sleep problems that make everything else harder to treat.

Addressed as part of the whole picture, not with sedatives by default.

If what you're facing isn't listed here, reach out anyway. A provider must complete a full evaluation before making any diagnosis or treatment recommendation — nothing on this page is medical advice.
